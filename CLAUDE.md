# Thread & Salt — engagement context (handoff for Claude Code)

This file is the full context for the Thread & Salt consulting engagement, exported from a
Cowork chat so work can continue in Claude Code. Read it top to bottom before doing anything.

## 1. The client

**Thread & Salt** — a small, coastal, minimalist **direct-to-consumer fashion brand** that also
has a **physical store**. Small runs of natural-fabric pieces; mostly repeat customers plus
discovery via social/Instagram. New drops go to the mailing list first.

- **Storefront:** threadandsalt.syntheticsignal.io (live catalogue — e.g. Corail Linen Wrap Dress
  £96 [bestseller], Salt-Washed Cotton Tee £38 [bestseller], Natural Canvas Tote £42, Wide-Brim
  Straw Hat £58, Oatmeal Knit Jumper £88).
- **Founders / contacts:** **Mara** (creative) and **Jonah** (ops). **Jonah is the point of
  contact** — warm, a little scattered, non-technical.
- **Systems today:** Xero for accounting (nobody looks at it properly), a messy Google Sheet of
  orders/sales in Drive (CSV export available), a hosted storefront platform.

## 2. The ask

- **Core (priority):** monthly, quarterly and annual **management accounts**, generated reliably
  and **automatically — without a founder having to remember**. Clean, repeatable, readable enough
  to act on. This is the thing that keeps Jonah up at night; solve it first.
- **Stretch:** a **proactive, Claude-driven sales-insight agent** — spots trends, at-risk lines,
  restock/discount signals and surfaces them *before* a founder would look. Pattern is
  **observe → analyse → recommend → repeat**, not a single clever prompt.

## 3. Hard constraints (do not violate)

- **Budget: £2,000–£5,000, tight at the top, fixed fee agreed upfront.** No hourly billing.
- **Must be dead simple to use.** A two-person shop will only adopt something effortless — the
  finished thing should require ~nothing from them month to month. They open a document; they do
  not operate software.
- **Data is deliberately messy** — inconsistent columns, blank cells. Cleaning it is part of the
  job, not an afterthought.
- **Xero:** don't assume it's connected. A sandbox can be provided on request — decide sheet-vs-Xero
  at kickoff.
- **Quality bar:** the Negative Zero management-accounts report — that's the style and rigour to hit.

## 7. OPEN ITEMS — reviewed as the client; fix these next

Highest-value gaps found in a client-perspective review of the proposal:

1. **Running costs after handover** — proposal quotes the build but not the ongoing subscription/
   compute that keeps the automation firing. Add: what it costs monthly and who pays.
2. **Support when it breaks** — support ends 29 Aug, but the promise is "runs itself forever."
   Add an optional low-cost care plan / quarterly check.
3. **Trusting AI-generated numbers** — add a line: calculations are deterministic, every figure
   reconciles to Xero, first report gets human sign-off.
4. **Data privacy/confidentiality** — one clear sentence: data stays theirs, not used to train
   models, GDPR-fine.
5. **Physical store** — proposal/sample are effectively online-only. Confirm whether in-store/POS
   sales are in scope and how they're captured; the "channel split" must include them.
6. **VAT** — at ~£640k revenue they're over the UK VAT threshold. State whether figures are ex-VAT
   and whether VAT reporting is in/out of scope.
7. **SKU/channel split not yet shown** — proposal promises "top SKUs and channel split" but the
   sample only shows revenue/margins, because only monthly totals were available. The order-line
   CSV (`data/hackathon-1/threadandsalt-sales-data.csv`, referenced in the workbook but **not yet
   obtained**) is needed. Get it early.

Internal note: "~6 working days" for cleaning + report + 3 automated cadences + a genuine agent is
optimistic — either pad it or scope the agent explicitly as "v1, one live signal."
